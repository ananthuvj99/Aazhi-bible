**L124 — Pens appeared in Balan's pocket because the prompt described a teammate's sheet, not the one attached; a "sliding shutter" swung upward; unattached far men came out black and in a clump** (s16b-sh18 v1, partial, 480p test)

Symptom:
1. Balan had two golden pens in his shirt pocket. His sheet on Aswanth's account has no pens: a faded blue short-sleeve shirt over a white vest, a green-and-grey checked lungi, sandals.
2. His face did not look like a real person in the dim front close-up.
3. The wooden shutter swung upward like a flap instead of sliding to the right.
4. The men on the deck, seen through the opening, were dark figures in black, sitting in a clump in front of the mast with a lantern on the floor, not in a ring round it.
5. His getting up did not move like a real body.

Cause:
1. The creators use different platform accounts, each with its own sheets. The prompt's text for Balan was copied from the repo card of Ranjith's `@balan` (full-sleeve shirt, two pens, white mundu, shoes); the attached sheet was Aswanth's `@balan-at-20s`. Text and image disagreed and the text's pens were drawn.
2. Very low light on a face ("unlit, dim") with no words about skin gave a waxy face; 480p adds to it.
3. "Slides sideways in a wooden groove" was not enough; the model chose the commonest shutter, a top-hinged flap.
4. The far men were written in words only ("five small seated men"), so they had no faces, clothes or light: silhouettes. "Round the mast" without geometry gave a clump.
5. Getting up was written as one verb.

Rule:
1. Describe a character only from the sheet that is attached on the creator's own account. When a creator registers his own version of a character, look at his image first; never copy the repo card of another account's sheet.
2. A face in low light still gets a readable soft light and a skin line: pores, marks from the sheet, natural eyes with a wet highlight, small face muscles moving.
3. A moving part gets its mechanism in its own block: what it is, what it runs in, the one direction it can move, and that it stays in its plane.
4. People seen far away are still attached when their look matters, with "in their own light-coloured clothes, lit by the lantern", and the group's geometry is written: one full circle all the way round the mast, the mast in the centre, evenly spaced, every man facing it.
5. Body movements are written with weight: what pushes, what settles, pauses; and awkward transitions (standing up) happen in the cut, off screen.
