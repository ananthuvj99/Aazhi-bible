**L132 — The other boat came close and big, its flare never fired, and our crowd waved at the sky the wrong way; our flare came out as a firework from a tube** (s16b-sh23 v1, partial)

Symptom: the small fishing boat sat about 30 m away, too big against our men; it never launched its flare; our men waved upward and to the side instead of toward it, and the strangers never waved back; Sasi held a tube and our flare burst like a pink-and-blue firework; the emotion (people meeting people at sea) did not land.

Cause:
1. "About 50 m off our right side" with no frame size let the boat come close.
2. Their flare was written only in the FLARES block and as "its pink flare rises and bursts" inside a wide beat, with no time and no launch from the boat.
3. The men's direction was "at the right-hand rail" and "toward the small boat", but the camera was not placed looking the same way, and the final beat put "faces lit blue from above", which turned faces to the sky.
4. "Bursts into a huge glowing star that crackles" read as a firework; "flare pistol" with "wide short barrel" read as a tube.

Rule:
1. A far object gets a distance AND a frame size ("about 400 m, near the horizon, about one twentieth of the frame's width") and "stays far away the whole shot".
2. Every action of a far element has its own timed moment in a beat ("At 2 s the pink flare rises from it").
3. For a group reacting to something, put the camera behind them looking the same way, and write in the beat and the locks that they all face it and wave to it, and that it answers.
4. Name the real thing and rule out the look-alike: "real signal flares, not fireworks"; "a short pistol the size of his hand, not a tube".
5. State the emotion in plain words in SCENE CONTEXT and PERFORMANCE ("people seeing other people after days of empty sea").
