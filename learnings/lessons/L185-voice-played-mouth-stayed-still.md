Symptom: Antony's line was heard but his lips and jaw stayed still, like a voice-over on a frozen face.
Cause: The prompt never asked for mouth movement; it said only "speaking, head steady". It had grown to about 8,400 characters (a long reference block, MEMORY listing things behind the camera), burying the dialogue instructions, and the "(most important)" block was about the face and hands, not the mouth. The framing was waist-up, so the face was small.
Rule: Every dialogue shot says the speaker's lips and jaw move visibly with every syllable from the first word to the last, in time with the voice, "never a voice-over over a still face", and the mouth comes to rest after the line. In a long or wide shot that block is the "(most important)" one, and the prompt is cut back (MEMORY to what the camera sees) until the dialogue instructions are not buried.
Checklist line: Moving mouth (L185): the speaker's lips and jaw are written to move with every syllable, in ACTION and POSITIVE LOCKS, with "never a voice-over"; in wide or long shots the mouth is the "(most important)" block; prompts over about 7,500 characters are cut back first.
