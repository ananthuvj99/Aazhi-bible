**L121 — The goodbye was rushed: every second was filled, so the feeling had no room** (3tree-d v1)

Symptom: the lines and cuts were right, but the parting felt hurried — they stood, spoke and he left. No moment where they just look at each other, no look back from the gate.

Cause: the prompt was timed only around the lines (12 s, five cuts, each cut barely longer than its line). An emotional beat — a goodbye, a decision, bad news — lives in the silences, and nothing in the prompt gave them time or said they mattered.

Rule:
1. Before timing a scene, ask what it is about emotionally and where the silence belongs (the look before parting, the pause at the door, the look back). Give each such silence its own seconds (2–3 s) and write it as an action ("silence: he keeps looking into her face… mouth closed").
2. For emotional scenes use the full 15 s rather than cutting to fit the lines; let the walk be slow and the pauses be written.
3. Say it in SCENE and PERFORMANCE: "the silences matter as much as the words; nobody hurries", and ask the ambience to be "a little more present in the silences".
