**L156: A far object keeps its size from the shot before; only the lens and real change grow it**

What happened: Ranjith asked for a bigger fire in SB029. The first end keyframes showed a big blaze with a huge smoke cloud, the boat a quarter, then a sixth of the frame wide. Ranjith: "previous shot din have this much fire and it showed more distance ryt". SB028 had ended on a tiny dot, so 10 seconds later the boat read about 150–200 m away and the fire had jumped in size.

Rule:
1. Read the far object's size in the last frame of the shot before (the SB028 dot: about a fortieth of the frame at 35mm).
2. Its new size = old size × (new lens mm ÷ old lens mm), as long as the distance doesn't change. Check it with the geometry: a 9 m boat at 500 m through 85mm on a 21:9 frame is about a twentieth of the frame; at 35mm about a fiftieth.
3. "Bigger" means growth that can happen in the shot's seconds (the fire catches the nets and flares, a smoke plume starts), never a jump. Write when it grows, by the second.
4. A distant boat sits on the horizon line, just under it, never well below it.
5. If the image model won't make it small enough, scale the object in the keyframe by hand and fill the sea back in from clean water, then QC the patch.
