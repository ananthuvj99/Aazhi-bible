Symptom: the shot opened on empty sea with a thin strip of horizon at the top; at 3.5–4.8 s the uru and its wake faded in on that horizon like a ghost, out of nothing, instead of being revealed by the camera.
Cause: the uru was 150 m straight ahead, so it belonged on the horizon that the start frame already showed; the start frame had an empty horizon, and the model could only reconcile it with the end frame by dissolving the uru in.
Rule: for a reveal, the subject must be truly out of frame at the start: keep its direction out of the start frame (camera tilted steeply down with no horizon, or turned away), and let the camera move bring it in from a frame edge. Write that the subject is solid and present from the first frame it is seen, never fading in. Never show the subject's place empty in a start frame.
Checklist line: reveals: the start frame does not show the place where the revealed subject stands; the subject enters from a frame edge through the camera move and is solid from its first frame; "never fades in or appears out of nothing" in CONSTRAINTS.
