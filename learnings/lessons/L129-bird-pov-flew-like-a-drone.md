**L129 — The view from a bird's back flew like a drone: no wingbeats, no banking, a white lump for a body, and it sank onto the deck** (s16b-sh20 v1, partial)

Symptom: the angle was right (camera on the bird's back, the uru and the crew below), but across all 15 s the wings never appeared or beat, the view never rolled into the turn, the "bird" at the bottom of the frame was a smooth white lump, and it glided slowly and steadily down to a few metres above the men, like a drone, instead of circling and flying on.

Cause: the prompt said "beating and gliding", "banks into a slow, wide circle" and "the deck passes under the bird's head" in general words. It did not give the bird's anatomy as seen from its back, the wingbeat rate and what each stroke looks like in frame, how a bird turns (it rolls), a forward speed, or a height limit. "The deck passes under the bird's head" pulled it down onto the deck.

Rule: for a creature point of view, write:
1. Its anatomy as seen from the camera: which parts are in frame, where, their shape and markings ("long, narrow, pointed wings at the left and right edges, always partly in frame").
2. The movement mechanics in numbers and in frame terms: beats per second, what the downstroke and upstroke do in the frame, the view bobbing with each beat; glides with stiff wings.
3. Turns as the animal does them: roll the body, inner wing low, outer wing high, horizon tilted by a stated angle, head level.
4. Speed and height limits: "never hovers, never drifts slowly", "never lower than 20 m".
5. A timeline that counts the beats in each section.
If it still moves like a drone, block the flight path in 3D (W19) or use a start frame.
