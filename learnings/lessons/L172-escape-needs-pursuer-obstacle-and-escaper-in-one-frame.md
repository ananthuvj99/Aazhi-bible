Symptom: the escape did not read. The camera travelled forward with the uru, passed the three motorboats, and they left the frame at the bottom by 8 s; the snag was an unclear splash and nobody saw the pursuers caught, swerve or fall behind. The uru drove straight between the vallams instead of turning round the nets, and the "nets" came out as Chinese lift nets on frames.
Cause: a camera that travels with the escaper outruns the pursuers; the prompt's "rising over the escape" let the model keep only the uru in frame. "Nets" without a type defaults to the most picturesque net (cheena vala).
Rule: when the story is an escape (or a chase that breaks), the pursuers, the obstacle and the escaper are all in one frame at the moment it happens: the camera stops travelling with the chase, holds its place or pulls back and rises, and lets the boats run away from it into the frame; the pursuers stay in frame to the end. Name the net type: flat drift nets lying in the water, marked only by lines of floats, no frames, no poles, no lift nets.
Checklist line: In an escape or chase-break shot, are the pursuers, the obstacle and the escaper all in frame at the moment of escape, and does the camera avoid outrunning the pursuers? Is the net type named (floats in the water, no frames or poles)?
