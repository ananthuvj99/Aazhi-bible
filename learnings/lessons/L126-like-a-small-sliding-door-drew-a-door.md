**L126 — "Like a small sliding door" drew a door: a comparison word is drawn as the thing itself** (s16b-sh18 v3, partial)

Symptom: everything in the take was right (Balan, the ring men, the sideways slide), but the small eye-level window came out as a tall plank door that he slides open with his whole hand.

Cause: the shutter block said the board runs in rails "like a small sliding door". The model took the comparison as the object (L27: everything written is a candidate to be drawn). Nothing said that the wall stays solid below, above and beside the opening, and the camera line "until the view fills most of the frame" asked for a big opening.

Rule:
1. Never describe an object by comparing it to a different object the shot must not show. Describe its own size and place.
2. For an opening in a wall, write the wall first (solid from floor to ceiling), then the opening's size in centimetres with a familiar size ("the size of a serving tray"), and say the wall is solid below it, above it and on both sides.
3. Keep the camera wording consistent with the size: look "through the small opening, the wall framing the view on all four sides", never "the view fills the frame".
