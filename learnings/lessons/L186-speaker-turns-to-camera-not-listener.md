Symptom: in the court take the lawyer pleaded to SI Ramachandran (frame right) instead of to the magistrate on the bench (Ranjith: "advocate is looking at si and speaking in take 1, it should be at judge right, didn't we have eyeline in prompt").
Cause: the prompt asked for two things that cannot both happen: EYELINES said "the lawyer: on the bench", but the beat said "facing the bench, his face in three-quarter view toward the camera" while the camera was BEHIND him (pushing up the room from the back benches). The model kept the face toward the camera and turned him sideways, so his eyes landed on the nearest person on that side, Ramachandran. Ramachandran's line had the same conflict: he spoke to the lawyer (Ranjith: "even SI is speaking to lawyer"). Ranjith on the 30 s multi-speaker take: "30 SECS SHOT DID NOT COME WELL".
Rule: for every line, place the camera on the LISTENER's side of the speaker, so that looking at the listener also shows the face. Write the eyeline inside the line's own beat ("speaks to the magistrate, eyes up on the bench at frame LEFT"), name who he must NOT look at ("never at Ramachandran"), and never ask for "face toward the camera" when the camera is behind the speaker. If the move cannot get in front of him in time, the line is heard over another face instead.
Also: with several speakers in one generation the model makes them talk to each other. The station-and-court trial of 30 s multi-speaker moving takes (s42-sh09, s44f-sh01) is dropped for dialogue: one speaker per generation again, each in a medium close-up shot from his listener's side, reactions as separate silent shots; 30 s takes stay for silent or single-speaker moments (s42-sh06).
Checklist line: each speaker's camera position is on his listener's side; the eyeline (who, and which side of frame) is written in the line's own beat and in EYELINES, with who he must not look at; never "face to camera" when the camera is behind the speaker (L186).
