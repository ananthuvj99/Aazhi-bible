**L123 — Every person in frame gets his own facial expression, written beat by beat** (s23-sh05 v4 and the SB031 shots)

Symptom: when Antony points out to sea, one of the men shows fear and another has no expression at all; the faces do not agree with the moment.

Cause: the prompts gave each man an action ("grips the rail", "shakes his head") and the scene one feeling ("fear in every face"), but never said what each face does. The model fills the gaps with a blank face.

Rule:
1. Every prompt has an EXPRESSIONS block (after ACTION) with one line per person in frame, including background and edge figures: eyes, brows, mouth and jaw, and how the expression changes with time ("0–2 s groggy, puffy eyes; from 3 s brows rising, alarm").
2. If ten people are in frame, ten lines. Faces seen from behind get their profile or body tension when they turn.
3. The expression fits the moment and the character (Raghavan's fear is an old man's tired dread, Balan's is tense and calculating); never the same words for two people.
4. Listeners' mouths stay closed during a line; their expression is in the eyes and brows.
