Symptom: in Part B the uru's wake ran straight back through the middle of the line of vallams, as if it had gone through the nets, though in Part A it had cut hard round the end of the line (Ranjith: "if you see the previous shot, we cut the fifth one and then come right, now we are showing it in the center"). Aashaan, who walked into the shot to watch the motorboats, looked at the sky and the camera instead. The motorboats were so small and far that nothing could be seen happening to them.
Cause: the start frame put the vallams dead astern with a straight wake; nothing tied the new angle to the path of the turn. Aashaan's eyeline was not locked. The action was set too far from the lens to read.
Rule: after a turn round an obstacle, the next angle keeps the obstacle on the side it was passed and the wake curving round it; check the start frame against the last frame of the previous part before writing. Lock every named person's eyeline to the thing they react to ("his eyes on the motorboats the whole time, never on the camera or the sky"). Put the action the shot is about close enough to read (motorboats about 20–30 m, not on the horizon).
Checklist line: Does the start frame keep the geometry of the previous shot (which side the obstacle was passed, the wake's curve)? Is each reacting person's eyeline locked to the thing they react to? Is the key action close enough to read?
