# Shot sizes, framings and camera angles

Every shot is four decisions: **size** (how much is in frame), **angle** (where the camera looks from), **lens / FOV** (how the space is drawn), and **movement** (how it moves — see `movement-library.md`). "Wide shot" or "close-up" is never a movement; it is a size, and a moving shot can change size as it travels (a push-in goes from MS to CU).

## 1. Shot sizes

| Size | Also called | In frame | Usual FOV | Feels like | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EWS | extreme wide, establishing | the place dominates; people tiny or absent | 107° or 84° (or 8° for a compressed far view) | scale, isolation, "where are we" | opening a sequence, landscapes, cities, battlefields |
| WS | wide, long shot | full figure with plenty of surroundings | 84° or 63° | a person in their world | entrances, exits, blocking |
| FS | full shot | head to toe, little around | 63° or 47° | body language, movement | walking, working on the beach |
| MWS | medium wide, cowboy, medium long | knees up | 47° | conversational distance with body | group talk, standing dialogue |
| MS | medium | waist up | 47° | neutral, everyday | most dialogue, actions with the hands |
| MCU | medium close-up | chest or shoulders up | 29° | closer, emotional but composed | dialogue singles, lip-sync shots |
| CU | close-up | the face fills the frame | 29° or 18° | emotion, thought | reactions, key lines |
| ECU | extreme close-up | eyes, mouth, a detail of the face | 18° or 12° | intensity, obsession | the moment of decision, a tear |
| Insert | detail shot | an object: a hand, a letter, a knot, a watch | 12° or macro | information, clue, ritual | props that matter to the story |

## 2. Size is distance plus FOV

The same close-up looks different depending on how it is made:

- **Wide lens, close camera** (a CU at 63° from 0.5 m) — intimate, a little distorted, the room still visible around the face. Feels present and slightly uneasy.
- **Long lens, far camera** (a CU at 18° from 4 m) — flattering, compressed, the background melts away. Feels observed from a distance.

Choose the combination for the feeling, then state both distance and FOV in the prompt.

## 3. Relational framings

| Framing | What it is | Use |
|---|---|---|
| single | one person in frame | dialogue coverage |
| clean single | one person, nobody else in frame | isolation, a private thought |
| dirty single | one person with a soft piece of another (a shoulder, an ear) in the foreground | keeps the two connected |
| two-shot | two people in frame | relationships, balance of power |
| three-shot, group shot | three or more | families, crews, classrooms |
| over-the-shoulder (OTS) | over one person's shoulder onto the other's face | standard dialogue; puts us beside the listener |
| reaction shot | the listener, not the speaker | where the emotion often really is |
| POV | what a character sees | identification |
| master shot | the whole scene in one wider shot | the safety shot every scene is built from |
| cutaway | something else in the location (a clock, the sky, a dog) | rhythm, time passing, hiding a cut |
| establishing shot | the location before the scene | orientation |

## 4. Camera height and vertical angle

| Angle | Camera | Feels like | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| eye level | at the subject's eyes, level | neutral, equal | the default for most drama |
| high angle | above, looking down | vulnerable, small, watched, judged | a character at their lowest, defeat, being judged |
| low angle | below, looking up | power, threat, heroism; or a child's view of adults | authority figures, villains, heroes, a parent seen by a child |
| bird's-eye / overhead | straight down, 90° | fate, pattern, detachment | a body, a table, a crowd pattern, a map-like chase |
| worm's-eye | on the ground, looking steeply up | monumental, unsettling | towers, giants, a figure looming |
| Dutch / canted | frame tilted on its axis | unease, a world off balance | sparingly; the dream, intoxication |
| ground level | lens at ground height, level | close to the earth, children, animals | feet, children, animals, water reaching the shore |
| hip level | at the waist | an observer at work level; Westerns' cowboy | hands working, standoffs |
| shoulder level | slightly above eye level | natural, gently dominant | over-the-shoulder coverage |

## 5. Horizontal direction

| Direction | Feels like | Use |
|---|---|---|
| frontal | confrontation, confession, address | a direct plea, an interrogation |
| three-quarter | natural, readable, the standard | most dialogue and lip-sync |
| profile | observing; the space between two people | two people at odds, a silent walk |
| from behind | mystery, following, the unknown face | leaving, arriving, following someone into the unknown |

## 6. Composition terms

- **Rule of thirds** — key elements on the thirds lines rather than dead centre.
- **Centred / symmetrical** — formal, ritual, stillness, authority; churches, courts, palaces (Kubrick, Wes Anderson).
- **Headroom** — space above the head; too little feels cramped, too much feels small.
- **Lead room (look room)** — space in front of where a character looks or moves.
- **Negative space** — large empty areas that make a person feel small or alone.
- **Frame within a frame** — a doorway, window, mirror or arch framing the subject; often used to show a character trapped or watched.
- **Foreground, midground, background** — depth in layers; the prompt writer wants them stated separately.
- **Short side / long side** — placing a face so it looks toward the near frame edge feels trapped; toward the open side feels free.

## 7. Writing it in the prompt

State every element as something measurable:

- size: "medium close-up, chest up";
- angle: "camera at 0.8 m, tilted up 15° toward his face" (height in metres, tilt in degrees);
- direction: "three-quarter view of her left side";
- lens: FOV in degrees from the anchor table only;
- distance: "2 m from him".

Example: "low-angle medium close-up, camera at 0.9 m tilted up 15°, three-quarter view of his right side, 29° FOV, 2 m from him."
