# Camera rulebook — template

Great films decide their camera grammar before shooting. Son of Saul's rules were one lens, always at the character's eye level, always with him. Shyju Khalid gave Nayattu restless movement and Joji steady tracking so the two films would never look alike. A rulebook keeps every scene of a project consistent, and makes the moments that break it land.

Fill this in with the director for each project. Keep it to a page. Every scene plan then starts by reading it.

---

## Template

```
CAMERA RULEBOOK — [project title]

1. ONE-LINE PHILOSOPHY
   The camera is [an observer / a companion / the protagonist's nervous system / a storyteller who knows more] because [reason].

2. DEFAULT GRAMMAR
   Height: [eye level / slightly low / child height …]
   Distance: [observer — wide and held / participant — wide lens close / neutral]
   Lens range: [e.g. 63°–29°; 18° only for …]
   How often the camera moves: [rarely — one significant move per scene / often / constantly]
   Dominant move family: [held frames + nature motion / slow pushes / floating walks / handheld …]
   Static frames: [what they're for in this film]
   Aspect ratio: […]
   Camera body: […] (heavy cinema / light)

3. CAMERA ARC (how the grammar changes as the story changes)
   Act / section 1: […]
   Turning point: [where and how the rules first break]
   Act / section 2: […]
   Climax: […]
   Ending: [the final image's grammar]

4. PER-CHARACTER STYLES (optional)
   [Character A]: [e.g. always shot still, eye level]
   [Character B]: [e.g. handheld when with A]

5. SIGNATURE MOVES AND THEIR BUDGET
   [Move] — allowed [once / only at …]
   e.g. Dolly zoom — once, at the midpoint revelation.
   e.g. 360° orbit — only in dream sequences.

6. BANNED MOVES (and why)
   e.g. No drone shots in the first act — the world must feel grounded and small.

7. LOCATION RULES
   [Location]: [rigs that fit the space; sight lines; which side of the axis]

8. TIME RULES
   Slow motion: [when allowed]
   Long takes: [when allowed]

9. AI GENERATION NOTES
   Model: […]  ·  Max duration per generation: […]
   Phrasings that work on this model: […]
   Phrasings that failed: […]
```

## Example — a fictional thriller

```
CAMERA RULEBOOK — "Night Ward" (a nurse on night shift realises a patient is being harmed)

1. ONE-LINE PHILOSOPHY
   The camera starts as a calm, professional observer like the nurse herself, and slowly becomes her racing mind.

2. DEFAULT GRAMMAR
   Height: eye level, 1.5 m. Distance: observer — medium shots, held.
   Lens range: 47°–29°; 18° only for monitors and hands.
   Movement: rare — one slow push per scene at most.
   Dominant family: locked frames with life in them (monitor lights, curtains, staff passing).
   Static frames mean routine and control. Aspect 2.39:1. Heavy full-frame cinema camera.

3. CAMERA ARC
   Act 1 (routine): held frames, symmetry, corridors shot square-on.
   First suspicion: the first creep forward — 0.2 km/h down the corridor.
   Act 2 (investigation): floating walks follow her; long lenses watch her from across the ward.
   Midpoint (proof): the one dolly zoom (dolly out + zoom in — the ward rushes in on her).
   Act 3 (confrontation): handheld for her, locked-off for the doctor.
   Ending: a locked wide of the empty ward at dawn; curtains moving in the air-con.

4. PER-CHARACTER STYLES
   The doctor: always still, centred, slightly low angle.
   The nurse: still in Act 1; moving from Act 2; handheld in Act 3.

5. SIGNATURE MOVES
   Dolly zoom — once (midpoint). Overhead top shot — only on the patient's bed, three times, each tighter.

6. BANNED MOVES
   No aerials, no whip pans, no slow motion: the film stays in real time and inside the hospital.

7. LOCATION RULES
   Corridors: centred one-point perspective, floating walk or dolly. Patient rooms: tripod and slider only (space).

8. TIME RULES
   Long takes allowed for her night rounds (one per act).

9. AI GENERATION NOTES
   [filled in during production]
```
