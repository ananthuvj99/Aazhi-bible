# Scene playbook — which camera strategy for which scene

Find the scene type, then adapt. These are starting points that great films have proven, not formulas. A film's own camera rulebook always wins over this file.

Each entry gives: **Strategy** (the camera's attitude) · **Typical moves** · **Avoid** · **References** · **Sketch** (a sample shot flow).

### Openings and establishing a world
- **Strategy:** tell the audience where they are, whose world it is and how the film will look. The first image sets the rules.
- **Typical moves:** a slow aerial or crane reveal; a long lateral truck through daily life; a static living frame with weather and people moving; a oner that walks us in.
- **Avoid:** generic drone swoops that could open any film.
- **References:** the helicopter following the car into the mountains in The Shining; Touch of Evil's crane oner; Moonlight's swirling 360°; Jallikattu's rapid montage of village life.
- **Sketch:** EWS aerial drifting forward → crane down into the street → follow the protagonist into frame.

### Entering a new world (seduction)
- **Strategy:** the camera leads us in step by step, so we're seduced along with the character.
- **Typical moves:** floating-walk lead or follow through doors and corridors; a oner that never lets us look away.
- **Avoid:** cutting, which breaks the spell.
- **References:** the Copacabana walk in Goodfellas.
- **Sketch:** lead them from the street → through the back corridor and kitchen → reveal the room as they arrive.

### Calm dialogue
- **Strategy:** let performance work. Matched singles, a stable axis, few moves.
- **Typical moves:** locked-off two-shot and matched over-the-shoulders; at most one slow push at the line that matters.
- **Avoid:** moving for the sake of moving; arcs during lines (they break lip-sync in AI).
- **Note:** when actors improvise, locked cameras on both sides let the performance run freely (The Wolf of Wall Street).
- **References:** Deakins' restraint; the coin-toss scene in No Country for Old Men.
- **Sketch:** two-shot → OTS A / OTS B at 29° → slow push on B at the turn.

### Tense confrontation
- **Strategy:** tighten the space as tension rises; widen the lens and shorten distance as things get dangerous.
- **Typical moves:** slow pushes on both sides; a slow arc in a confined space; tighter sizes as the scene goes on; a static frame on the calm person against handheld on the frightened one.
- **Avoid:** releasing tension with a wide too early.
- **References:** the arc in the cramped office in The Dark Knight; Mills handheld versus John Doe static in Seven.
- **Sketch:** wide two-shot → OTS singles → arc 60° as voices rise → tight CUs, one static, one handheld.

### Interrogation and power
- **Strategy:** angles and stillness carry power. The one in control is still; the one losing control moves.
- **Typical moves:** high angle on the questioned, low angle on the questioner; locked frames; a slow creep in on the suspect.
- **Avoid:** equal treatment if power is unequal.
- **References:** Seven's split styles; Parasite's vertical framing of class.

### Confession
- **Strategy:** intimacy and time. Hold long enough for the truth to arrive.
- **Typical moves:** a single slow push-in, or a locked close-up that doesn't cut away.
- **Avoid:** cutaways that let the audience off the hook.
- **References:** the slow push on Michael in The Godfather's restaurant (decision rather than confession, but the same grammar).

### Argument
- **Strategy:** energy and rhythm, cut or whipped between speakers.
- **Typical moves:** whip pans between them; handheld; overlapping action.
- **References:** the whip pans in Whiplash's confrontations.
- **Sketch:** handheld two-shot → whip to A → whip to B → hold on the one who goes silent.

### Realisation, decision, point of no return
- **Strategy:** the one significant move of the scene. Push into the mind at the instant it changes.
- **Typical moves:** slow push-in to CU; a rack focus to the thing that triggers it; dolly zoom only if it is the turning point of the film.
- **References:** The Godfather restaurant push; dolly zooms in Vertigo and Jaws.

### Reveal of a place
- **Strategy:** hold back, then open up. Start tight or blocked, end wide.
- **Typical moves:** crane or aerial rising reveal; pan or tilt reveal; pull-out; foreground wipe.
- **References:** the street folding overhead, tilted up to in Inception.

### Reveal of a person or threat
- **Strategy:** control what the audience sees and when.
- **Typical moves:** foreground wipe; rack focus to the background; slow pan that lands on them; POV.
- **Avoid:** showing them before the reveal.
- **References:** Halloween's POV opening.

### Farewell and departure
- **Strategy:** the camera chooses to stay behind with the one who remains.
- **Typical moves:** follow-then-hold; static wide as one walks away and shrinks; pull-out; a slow push on the one left behind.
- **Avoid:** following the one who leaves (unless it's their story).
- **Sketch:** two-shot → push on the one staying → static wide over their shoulder as the other walks away.

### Reunion
- **Strategy:** closing distance, then release.
- **Typical moves:** a lead track as one approaches; an arc (≤ 90°) as they embrace; a spiral or crane rise for an epic reunion.
- **References:** the 360° grammar from Moonlight and The Avengers, scaled down.

### Romance
- **Strategy:** weightless, flowing, the world falling away.
- **Typical moves:** floating walk, slow arcs, whip pans for playful energy, shallow focus, slow motion sparingly.
- **References:** La La Land's whip pans and flowing oners.

### Grief and death
- **Strategy:** stillness and distance; let nature move.
- **Typical moves:** locked frames with rain, wind and water moving; wide shots that make people small; very slow creeps; no showy moves.
- **Avoid:** pushing into tears; music-video moves.
- **References:** Ee.Ma.Yau's rain, fog and sea during a death in a coastal village; Roma's held wides.

### Loneliness and isolation
- **Strategy:** make space around the person; let the camera leave them.
- **Typical moves:** pull-out; static wide with negative space; crane or aerial rise; locked frame that traps.
- **References:** the camera retreating from Arthur on the stairwell in Joker; the trapped soldiers on the beach in Dunkirk.

### Fear, dread, horror
- **Strategy:** slow, gliding, inevitable; withhold.
- **Typical moves:** low-mode glides; slow zoom in; slow push down corridors; static frames with space where something could appear.
- **References:** the corridors and the slow zoom in The Shining; the corridor tracking in Elephant.

### Supernatural presence
- **Strategy:** make the space itself feel wrong, while the character stays fixed.
- **Typical moves:** dolly zoom; slow zoom in; slow roll; a static frame where the background seems to breathe.
- **References:** the dolly zoom on the road as the dark rider approaches in The Fellowship of the Ring; the slow zooms of The Shining.

### Suspense
- **Strategy:** show the audience what the character doesn't know; hold and creep.
- **Typical moves:** omniscient reveals (rack focus to the threat behind them); slow creeps; inserts that are big because they matter.
- **References:** Hitchcock's rule — a growing object is a growing threat.

### Chase
- **Strategy:** speed and geography. Keep screen direction consistent so the audience knows who is where.
- **Typical moves:** chase tracking, camera car, aerial follow, low skim, handheld running; occasional overhead for the map.
- **References:** Jallikattu's running camera and overhead torch-lit crowd; Mad Max: Fury Road.
- **Avoid:** breaking screen direction without a visible move.

### Fight and violence
- **Strategy:** decide: inside the fight (handheld, close, wide lens) or outside it (static, cold, unflinching).
- **Typical moves:** handheld with impact jolts; a long take for real time; or a locked frame that refuses to look away.
- **References:** Children of Men's long takes; 12 Years a Slave's static hold.

### Crowd chaos
- **Strategy:** be inside the mass; let the camera be jostled.
- **Typical moves:** handheld running in sync; long takes through the crowd; occasional overhead for scale.
- **References:** Jallikattu.

### Power shift
- **Strategy:** the camera flips with the power.
- **Typical moves:** roll (the new master ends upright, or the whole order tilts); angle change from low to high; a moving overhead; one character stops moving while the other starts.
- **References:** the Joker roll in The Dark Knight; the slow roll as Killmonger takes the throne in Black Panther; the slight push on Michael when he steps up in The Godfather.

### Rise and fall
- **Strategy:** vertical grammar.
- **Typical moves:** boom up with hope, down with failure; stairs; high and low angles that swap as fortunes swap.
- **References:** Full Metal Jacket's obstacle course boom; the staircases of Parasite.

### Memory and flashback
- **Strategy:** a distinct grammar from the present: distance, softness, or the remembered child's height.
- **Typical moves:** slow lateral pans; wide held frames; floating drift; match moves into and out of the memory.
- **References:** Roma's distant, empathetic wides and slow pans.

### Dream, hallucination, intoxication
- **Strategy:** break the film's normal rules on purpose.
- **Typical moves:** slow orbits, roll, body-mount, slow motion, dolly zoom, impossible fly-throughs, floating drift.
- **References:** the body-mount sequences in Requiem for a Dream; Moonlight's swirling camera.

### Trauma and ordeal
- **Strategy:** lock the audience to the character's experience.
- **Typical moves:** follow-behind-the-head, shallow focus, wide lens very close, long takes.
- **References:** Son of Saul; The Revenant.

### Comedy
- **Strategy:** timing and clarity; the camera stays out of the joke's way.
- **Typical moves:** static symmetrical frames; deadpan holds; whip pans and crash zooms for punchlines; precise trucks.
- **References:** Wes Anderson's pans and trucks; Tarantino's crash zooms; the satirical handheld zooms of The Big Short.

### Song, musical and montage
- **Strategy:** the camera dances with the music; cut and move on the beat.
- **Typical moves:** whip pans on the beat; flowing oners; cranes for the chorus; speed ramps (split into shots for AI).
- **References:** La La Land; Whiplash.

### Journey
- **Strategy:** momentum and landscape.
- **Typical moves:** side tracks, aerial follows, car mounts, timelapse for long distance.

### Work, ritual and labour
- **Strategy:** respect the craft; show the hands and the rhythm.
- **Typical moves:** inserts; slow motion on the decisive gesture; lateral trucks along the workers; locked frames that let the work play out.
- **References:** Chemmeen's fishermen at sea; Kurosawa's working groups.

### Water and sea
- **Strategy:** the water is a character. Ride it, or watch it.
- **Typical moves:** boat-riding with horizon roll, close to the actors; waterline dips; surf-line low; aerial top-down on a boat; wide held frames on the shore.
- **References:** Amaram's small-boat closeness; Chemmeen's sea moods; Moonlight's swimming lesson; Roma's beach long take.

### Night
- **Strategy:** darkness is a choice; light sources define the frame.
- **Typical moves:** slow drifts toward lamp-lit faces; static frames with a single practical light; handheld with torches.
- **References:** Ee.Ma.Yau's night with very little artificial light; Jallikattu's torch-lit chases.

### Final shot
- **Strategy:** the last image is the film's last word.
- **Typical moves:** pull-out or crane rise (release, consequence); a held static frame (the question left open); a slow push (the character's inner state).
- **References:** Catch Me If You Can's final pull-out.
