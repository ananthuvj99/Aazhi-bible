# Character voices — AAZHI

Each character gets a **speech fingerprint**: sentence length, register, filler habit, and one thing they never do. The "never" is usually what makes a voice recognisable.

---

## ANTONY — 1998 (age ~24)

- **Length:** short, tumbling, often unfinished because he is laughing or already moving.
- **Register:** pure coastal spoken. Zero formality, even to elders — warmth substitutes for deference.
- **Fillers:** heavy — `എടാ`, `പിന്നെ`, `അല്ലേ`, `ന്നാ`.
- **Habit:** teases before he answers. Deflects sincerity with a joke, then says the sincere thing anyway.
- **Never:** completes a formal sentence. Never says `ആയിരിക്കുന്നു`, `എന്നാൽ`, or anything with `-കുകയാണ്`.

> നീയവ്ടെ നിന്നോ, ഞാൻ വരാ. — അല്ല, ശരിക്കും വരാ ട്ടോ.

## ANTONY — 2026 (post-prison)

The most important voice in the film. Built by **subtraction** from 1998 Antony.

- **Length:** very short. Four to seven words. Often a single word.
- **Register:** still coastal — prison did not educate him, it silenced him. Same vocabulary, less of it.
- **Fillers:** gone. This is the loudest signal of the change. No `എടാ`, no `അല്ലേ`, no laughing.
- **Habit:** asks questions instead of asserting. Lets other people fill silence and finish their own thoughts into a trap.
- **Vocabulary is frozen in 1998.** He does not have the post-2000 English loanwords in his mouth. When he does use one, it lands slightly over-precise — learned from print, not from people. Use this sparingly, maybe three or four times in the film, at moments where someone notices.
- **Never:** raises his voice. Never uses an exclamation. Never repeats himself.

> അതെയോ.
> ...
> ആരാ പറഞ്ഞേ?

**Direction note for the script:** where 1998 Antony would have a four-line speech, 2026 Antony has a question and a silence. Write the silence as a beat, not as a line.

## CLARA

- **Length:** medium. She finishes her sentences — one of the few who does.
- **Register:** coastal but a step more schooled than Antony. She corrects his grammar occasionally, affectionately, in 1998.
- **Fillers:** light. `ട്ടോ` and `അല്ലേ` mainly.
- **Habit:** says the hard thing plainly while everyone else circles it.
- **2026:** the same directness, but slowed by caution. She now checks the room before she speaks. The correction habit is gone.
- **Never:** pleads. Even at the worst moment she states rather than begs.

## BALAN

- **Length:** long in company, clipped alone.
- **Register:** coastal, but he reaches for slightly grander words when he wants to be taken seriously — and gets them slightly wrong. This is his tell.
- **Fillers:** `ചേട്ടാ` used as a tool, over-frequently, on people who are not his elders.
- **Habit:** agrees, then undercuts. `ശരിയാ ചേട്ടാ... പക്ഷേ...` is his default shape.
- **Never:** states a direct opinion he can be held to. Everything is attributed to someone else — `ആള്ക്കാര് പറയാ...`

## JAISON FERNANDES

- **Length:** padded. Says in twenty words what needs five.
- **Register:** 2026 corporate Malayalam — heavy, unselfconscious English code-mixing mid-sentence.
- **Fillers:** English ones. `actually`, `basically`, `see`.
- **Habit:** uses the plural `നമ്മൾ` to include people who have not agreed to anything.
- **Never:** silent. He cannot tolerate a pause, which is precisely how Antony works him.

> See ചേട്ടാ, actually നമ്മള് ഈ deal onnu close ചെയ്താ, പിന്നെ എല്ലാം smooth ആ.

## RAMACHANDRAN

- **Length:** measured, complete, controlled.
- **Register:** **formal Malayalam, deliberately.** `ആയതിനാൽ`, `പ്രകാരം`, `സംബന്ധിച്ച്`. This is the one place literary Malayalam belongs in the film, and it should feel like a closing door.
- **Fillers:** none. He does not need them.
- **Habit:** answers a question with a procedural fact.
- **Never:** uses a sea word. Never uses a kinship term. He has no community register at all — that absence is the characterisation.

## PROF. VARKEY

- **Length:** varies — he is the only character allowed a genuine paragraph, and only two or three times in the film.
- **Register:** educated but warm. He can reach for a formal construction and then immediately undercut it in plain speech. That movement between registers is his voice.
- **Fillers:** `മ്ം` — he listens more than he talks.
- **Habit:** teaches by question. Rarely gives Antony an answer directly.
- **Never:** condescends. His formality is generosity, not distance — the exact inverse of Ramachandran's.

**Design note:** Varkey and Ramachandran both speak formal Malayalam. The difference is that Varkey moves out of it toward the listener and Ramachandran never does. Play the pair deliberately.

## SELVAN

- Tamil-first speaker. Malayalam is broken, mixed with Tamil, spoken through exhaustion and salt water.
- Short, fragmented, repeated. Trailing off.
- **Never:** speaks a grammatically complete Malayalam sentence. His one clear utterance should be the address on the letter, and it should be Tamil.
