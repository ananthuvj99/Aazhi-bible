SCENE CONTEXT
Hazy morning, 1998, the wide calm water of the channel leaving Cochin harbour, Kerala, between two low palm-lined shores. Emotion (one, for the whole shot): excited. One continuous shot, 10 seconds: Antony's big wooden cargo uru sails away from the view toward the open sea, its wake spreading back toward the camera, and the two shores slowly fall back past the edges of the frame; the voyage has begun. Nobody speaks.

MEMORY (the departure; the same as the panel-15 shot s13-sh06)
Place: the channel exactly as in the reference: calm green-grey water with a light ripple; on the left and right edges low shores of leaning coconut palms, their trunks grey and curved, their crowns dark green against the haze; under the palms a few small houses with red clay-tiled roofs and white or ochre walls, two or three wooden canoes pulled up on the mud bank on each side; ahead, beyond the uru, the channel opening out to hazy open sea and a pale, soft horizon.
The uru: seen from directly behind in the centre of the water: a weathered blue-grey hull streaked with long rust stains and a red-oxide band at the waterline; one tall wooden mast with its rigging; the carved two-deck wooden deckhouse with turned-wood railings at the stern, facing the view; the word COCHIN painted in white capital letters across the stern exactly as in the reference.
Light: hazy morning sun, about 5000K, from the left of the view; the haze over the far water and the shores about 6500K.
People in frame: none readable; the crew aboard are too far and too small to be seen.
NOW: the uru about 60 m ahead of the camera, already under way, stern to the view; its wake reaching back toward the camera.

ACTIVE REFERENCES

Same uru, same shores, same houses, same sky. 100% matches the reference.

Its wake opens in a clean V about 20 degrees wide behind the stern: two arms of low rolling waves, about 20–30 cm high, white-edged near the stern and fading to smooth glassy ridges by the time they reach the camera about 60 m back; between the arms, a flattened, slightly churned strip of water with small bubbles and foam from the hull, about 6 m wide at the stern and 15 m wide at the camera. The arms reach the camera at about 6 s and gently lift the view about 10 cm. The wake catches the low sun as moving lines of glitter. FORMAT MODE

7.5s to 10.0s — Hold: the uru about a third smaller than at the start, still sailing straight for the open sea, the channel widening around it, the wake settling into long glittering ridges.

THE DEPARTURE (most important)
The feeling of freedom lives only in the movement: the uru keeps a perfectly steady, straight course and speed, never turning, never stopping; its mast stays upright, swaying only a degree or two with the swell; its stern lifts and settles about once every 4 s; the wake keeps spreading evenly from its stern; the shores open out to the sides as it passes between them, and the sea ahead grows wider and brighter. No sails suddenly unfurling, no flags, no people waving from the deck, no birds flying in formation, no sun flare bursting across the frame, no dolphins, no second boat.

EYELINES

Nothing moves by itself; every movement has a cause.
