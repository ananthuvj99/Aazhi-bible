Afternoon, 1998, a small police station on the Kollam coast of Kerala, the Sub-Inspector's office. Two men from the fishing harbour have brought a written complaint and an envelope and laid them on his desk. One continuous shot, 10 seconds: Sub-Inspector Ramachandran alone in a medium close-up behind his desk, reading the two pinned sheets of the complaint, looks up over them at the man across the desk and asks one short doubting question in Malayalam. Only Ramachandran speaks.

Place: a small, plain 1998 Kerala police-station room about 3.5 m by 4 m: walls lime-washed pale green to shoulder height with a thin dark green line, dirty cream above, the plaster flaking in patches; a red oxide cement floor worn pale in the middle; one barred window in the wall to the right of the desk, 1 m wide, five vertical iron bars painted black, wooden shutters folded open, the afternoon sky white beyond it; a tall grey steel almirah against the back wall at the left, its paint chipped at the handle; a stack of old buff files tied with red cloth tape on top of it; a ceiling fan with three brown blades turning slowly overhead, out of frame, its moving shadow faint on the back wall; a heavy dark wooden desk, its top worn smooth, with a sheet of glass on it.
Light: about 3:00 p.m.; daylight from the barred window at the right; one bare tungsten bulb hanging off frame above the desk, switched on, weak in the daylight. No pictures, no calendars, no notices, no lettering on any wall.
People in frame: only Ramachandran.
Out of frame: Balan sits in the one wooden chair across the desk, just left of the lens; Jaison stands behind Balan's chair at his left shoulder, further left. Neither is ever seen in this shot.

ACTIVE REFERENCES

The complaint: two sheets of white foolscap paper, 33 cm by 21 cm, pinned together at the top-left corner by one steel pin, covered in close blue-ink handwriting on both sides; he holds them at chest height in both hands, the written side toward himself, so the camera sees only the back of the second sheet, where the blue writing shows through faintly as soft, mirror-reversed grey lines that can never be read. The hardest thing is a policeman who thinks a complaint is foolish and lets the man see it, without a sneer; show it only in small movements. 0.0s-2.4s: he reads; his eyes move left to right along a line twice, then stop; at 1.6s his eyebrows draw together a few millimetres and he breathes out through his nose, once, short. 2.4s-3.0s: he lowers the sheets about 15 cm, so they rest at the bottom of the frame, and lifts his eyes, then his head about 10 degrees, to Balan just left of the lens; his head is steady from 3.0s. 3.0s-5.0s: he speaks the line, flat and slow, one eyebrow (his right, on frame left) lifting by a hair on the last word; the papers stay still. 5.0s-10.0s: silent, he holds Balan's eyes, waiting; at 6.4s he tilts his head 5 degrees and lets the pinned sheets drop the last 3 cm onto the desk glass with a soft slap; at 8.0s his right hand comes to rest flat on top of them; his gaze sharpens slightly as he listens.
