## Master prompt
```text
SCENE CONTEXT
A dark night out on the open sea off Kerala, about 1985, in a dream. A father and his son of about 10 are alone in a small wooden vallam; far ahead of them another small boat is going down, and one weak torch light on it flickers. The boy, kneeling at the bow, springs up to jump into the water toward it; his father lunges forward and grips his wrist and holds him back, as the far light dies. Emotion: scared. The shot in one sentence: a locked, low side view of the whole small boat across the water, the bow at the right, the far light at the right on the horizon. Nobody speaks.

MEMORY (the dream at sea; the same words in every shot of SB012–SB013)
Place: open sea at night, no land anywhere, a dark navy sea with a long low swell and small breaking caps; a very dark navy sky with a few faint stars, no moon.
Our boat: one small plank vallam about 7 m long and 1.2 m wide, low and narrow, black-tarred planks with a faded blue band along the top edge, a raised curved bow at the right, two wooden thwarts, two long wooden oars lying along the bottom, a coil of coir rope, one brass hurricane lantern standing on the bottom boards near the stern at the left.
The other boat: one small vallam about 60 m ahead of the bow, at the right of the frame on the horizon, its stern already under water, its bow tilted up about 30 degrees in black silhouette; one weak white torch light on its bow.
People in frame: the boy Antony, about 10: lean, sun-browned, short black hair with a cowlick at the crown, a faded sky-blue half-sleeve cotton shirt with one button missing, khaki half trousers, a thin black cord with a small aluminium cross medal. His father, about 40: tall, broad-shouldered, sun-darkened, short black hair greying at the temples, thick black moustache, a faded white cotton vest, a faded blue checked lungi tied up at the knee, a faded white thorthu tied round his head, a thin silver chain with a small cross.
NOW: the boy kneels on the bottom boards just behind the bow, both hands on the gunwale, staring at the far light at the right; the father sits on the rear thwart behind him in the middle of the boat, both hands on one oar laid across the gunwales, staring the same way.

ACTIVE REFERENCES
None attached. The two boats, the father, the boy, the sea and the lights are described in full in this prompt; build them only from these words. Exactly two people in the shot, each once.

THE FAR LIGHT
One small white torch light on the bow of the sinking boat, about 60 m away: a point about 1/200 of the frame width, never a beam toward us, never a fire, never a flare. Its reflection a short broken white streak on the water below it. 0.0s–4.0s: steady but weak, swaying as the far boat rolls. 4.0s–6.0s: it flickers, dims and brightens twice. 6.0s–8.0s: it dims to a faint dot. 8.0s: it goes out. 8.0s–12.0s: nothing at that place: the black silhouette of the tilted bow, then only dark water as it slides under at 10.5s. The far boat is about one tenth of the frame width and never bigger.

THE GRIP (most important)
Small, real, observable movements only, one person moving at a time. 0.0s–2.5s: hold: the boy kneels still at the bow, both hands on the gunwale, leaning toward the light; the father still on the thwart. 2.5s–3.5s: the light flickers; the boy's shoulders lift; he pushes up off the gunwale onto his feet and puts his left foot up on the gunwale, his body leaning out over the water toward the right, his left arm reaching back for balance. 3.5s–4.5s: the father drops the oar into the boat, lunges forward off the thwart onto one knee, and his LEFT hand closes round the boy's LEFT wrist; the four fingers wrap the wrist, the thumb on the inside, the knuckles pale. 4.5s–6.0s: the boy pulls once against the grip toward the light, his arm straightening; the father's arm holds, his elbow bent, his weight back. 6.0s–7.5s: the father draws the boy's wrist down and toward him; the boy's foot comes off the gunwale and he drops back down onto his knees in the boat, facing the light. 7.5s–12.0s: hold: the father on one knee behind him, still holding the wrist, the two of them staring at the place where the light was; at 8.0s the light goes out; the boy's free right hand grips the gunwale. The father never lets go. Avoid: the boy jumping or falling in, a hug, any hand toward the lens, a third arm, a wrist that changes side.

FORMAT MODE
One continuous shot, 12 seconds, real time, no cut, no slow motion. 21:9 cinematic. Runs the full 12 seconds.

CAMERA AND ACTION
0.0s–12.0s: the camera is on the water about 7 m off the boat's left side, about 1.2 m above the sea, level, looking straight across the boat; lens about 35mm; a wide shot: the whole vallam across the middle of the frame, bow at the right, stern at the left, the father and the boy full figure; the horizon about one third down from the top of the frame; the far boat and its light at the right of the frame on the horizon, just past the bow. Sharp: the two people and our boat; the far boat slightly soft. The camera rises and falls gently with the swell; otherwise locked.
Action as written in THE GRIP. Nobody else is anywhere; nothing else on the sea.

EYELINES
The boy: 0.0s–12.0s at the far light at the right of the frame, gaze level with the horizon, head turned to frame right in profile, never back at his father, even while his wrist is held; at 8.0s, when the light goes out, his eyes search a little left and right of that place on the horizon. The father: 0.0s–3.5s at the far light at the right, level with the horizon; 3.5s–6.0s down at the boy's wrist in his hand and the boy's back; 6.0s–12.0s at the far light again over the boy's head, level with the horizon. Nobody looks at the camera.

CAPTURE IMPERFECTIONS
The frame rises and falls about 20 cm with the swell, about once every 5 seconds; a slight jolt in the frame at 3.6s as the father lunges and the boat rocks; the boy's quick breaths visible in his shoulders from 2.5s; a small exposure dip at 8.0s when the far light goes out; a focus breath toward the far boat at 4.0s and back to the boat by 4.6s; the lantern flame jumps when the boat rocks.

REAL-WORLD PHYSICS
The vallam, about 400 kg of wood, rolls about 8 degrees toward the bow side when the boy stands with a foot on the gunwale at 3.0s and about 10 degrees back when the father lunges forward at 3.6s; water slops along the hull. The swell is about 1 m, long and slow, every 5 seconds; small caps break white in the wind. A sea wind of about 18 km/h from the right lifts the boy's shirt and the end of the father's thorthu. The far boat sinks stern first because it is full of water: its bow rises to about 30 degrees, then slides under at 10.5s, leaving a small swirl. The lantern on the bottom boards sways in its wire handle with the roll. Nothing moves by itself; every movement has a cause.

PERFORMANCE
A child who acts before he thinks; a father who knows the sea will take both of them. The boy's whole body pulls toward the light; the father's whole body anchors. No two people move at the same moment. Eyes never on the lens. Real skin, five fingers on every hand, wet spray on the arms. No posing, no acting to the camera.

LIGHTING
Low-key night. Exactly two lights: the one brass hurricane lantern on the bottom boards near the stern, about 2000K, a warm low glow on the inside of the boat, the father's legs and arm and the boy's back; the far torch about 5000K, a tiny white point on the horizon with its short reflection. Everything else lit by cold, dim starlit night about 7000K: the sea dark navy with faint grey highlights on the caps, the figures readable as dark shapes with warm edges from the lantern. Never flat, never bright blue, no moon.

AUDIO
Sound effects only — natural on-location sound, the soundtrack carries no music. No music at all: no score, no song, no humming, no cinematic sound design, no riser, no heartbeat effect.
No spoken words and no voices; nobody speaks; only breathing and effort sounds.
0.0s–12.0s: wind over the water, the slap of the swell on the planks, the creak of the thwarts; 3.0s a bare foot on the wooden gunwale; 3.6s the oar dropping into the boat with a hollow knock; 4.0s–6.0s the boy's quick breaths and a small cry of effort with the mouth closed; the hull knocking as it rocks; far off, faint, the gurgle of water as the other boat goes down at 10.5s.

STYLE
Photoreal Kerala period feature film, about 1985, 24fps, fine 35mm grain, gentle halation around the lantern and the far light, visible skin texture and real pores, no smoothing. Shot on a Sony Venice 2 full-frame digital cinema camera: very wide dynamic range that holds the brightest light and the deepest shade in one frame; clean, low-noise shadows; soft, gradual highlight roll-off; accurate, natural colour with rich, true skin tones; gentle filmic contrast; fine, organic texture; no over-sharpening, no glossy or video look. Low-key deep navy night with one warm lantern and one tiny white light; no dream filters, no blur effects.

CONSTRAINTS
Known failures for this kind of shot: the far light coming close and big (L132, L156) — it stays a tiny point on the horizon; a "hand with a torch" drawn by the camera (L150) — the torch is only a far point of light; a second lantern (L155) — exactly one; the far boat catching fire — it does not burn; the wrong arm or hand (L160) — the father's LEFT hand on the boy's LEFT wrist; the boy pointing while looking elsewhere (L162); a calm, slow mood in an emergency (L141) — the movements are urgent; shore lights or palms at sea (L78). Exactly two boats, two people. No lettering. No cut, no zoom.

POSITIVE LOCKS
Exactly two people, each once: the boy at the bow, the father behind him. Exactly one small vallam of ours, one sinking vallam far off at the right, one lantern, one far torch light that goes out at 8.0s. The father's left hand grips the boy's left wrist from 4.0s to the end and never lets go. The boy does not go in the water. Nobody speaks. Runs the full 12 seconds.
```
