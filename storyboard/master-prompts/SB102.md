```text
SCENE CONTEXT
Late afternoon, 2026, a small rented house in a coastal town in Kerala. A woman who has kept letters for almost thirty years shows them to a stranger who came to ask, and comes to the one paper she has never been able to open. One continuous medium close-up of Swathi alone in her cane chair, 15 seconds. Swathi speaks one line in Malayalam; nobody else speaks.

MEMORY (Swathi's sitting room, the same in every shot of this panel)
Place: a small sitting room about 3 x 3.5 m; walls painted a faded pale blue, a damp stain low in one corner; a cement floor polished red; behind Swathi on the wall at frame right ONE small round wall clock, 25 cm across, with a plain white dial and thin black hands, and below it a narrow wooden shelf with one steel water jug; at frame left behind her a narrow wooden window with green-painted bars and a half-drawn faded cotton curtain, late light outside; a low wooden table in front of her.
Light: late afternoon sun low outside the window at frame left, 4500K warming toward 3800K, coming in soft through the curtain; one weak tube light on the wall out of frame, off.
People in frame: only Swathi.
Swathi: about 50, slim, wheatish-brown skin with fine lines around her eyes and mouth and slight dark circles; black hair with grey streaks at the temples, centre parting, tied in a low bun; small gold stud earrings; a thin black-bead and gold chain; a plain pale lavender cotton saree with a thin dark-purple border, pallu over her LEFT shoulder and tucked at the waist, matching blouse; one thin gold bangle on her RIGHT wrist, a black-strap wristwatch on her LEFT wrist.
Off screen (never seen): Muthu sits on a plastic chair across the low table, just right of the camera.
NOW: Swathi sits forward on the edge of a cane chair; on the low table before her, at the bottom of the frame, lie the untied bundle of letters, a few unfolded, and on top of them the old brown envelope; her hands rest in her lap.

ACTIVE REFERENCES
No reference images are attached for this shot. Swathi is built only from the words in MEMORY: the one woman in frame, seated in the cane chair, who lays her hand on the old envelope and speaks. Nobody else is in frame.

THE LETTERS AND THE HOSPITAL ENVELOPE
About twelve old airmail letters: thin pale-blue aerogramme sheets folded in three, soft and furred at the folds, the ink faded to brown, a strip of foreign stamps on some, the handwriting far too small and soft to read; the white cotton string that tied them lies loose beside them. On top of them one brown paper envelope, 25 x 18 cm, the paper gone soft and yellow at the edges, one corner torn and mended with old yellowed tape; its flap is tucked in and has never been opened. All of it lies on a low wooden table 45 cm high at the bottom of the frame. Swathi touches only the envelope, with the fingertips of her RIGHT hand (on frame left, since she faces the camera); she does not lift it and does not open it. No writing on any paper is readable.

FORMAT MODE
One continuous shot, 15 seconds, real time, no cut, no slow motion. 21:9 cinematic widescreen. Runs the full 15 seconds.

CAMERA AND ACTION
The camera sits 1.6 m in front of Swathi across the low table, at her seated eye height about 1.1 m, on an 85mm lens, directly beside where Muthu sits, so her eyeline passes just to the right of the lens. Medium close-up: from just above her head to the middle of her chest, the edge of the table and the envelope just showing at the bottom of the frame; her face filling about 40% of the frame height, a little left of centre; the wall clock and shelf soft over her left shoulder at frame right, the barred window soft at frame left. Sharp: Swathi's eyes and face all through; the wall soft. The camera is locked; it only breathes.
0.0s to 3.0s (hold): Swathi sits forward, hands in her lap, looking at Muthu just right of the lens; she has just been asked if she saw the baby; she does not answer yet.
3.0s to 5.6s: her eyes go down to the envelope at the bottom of the frame; her right hand comes forward and her fingertips rest flat on it; they stay there; she breathes in once, slowly.
5.6s to 6.0s: she raises her eyes back to Muthu, just right of the lens; her head comes to rest and stays still; her hand stays on the envelope.
6.0s to 13.0s: she speaks her one Malayalam line, slow and quiet, in three parts with short pauses, head steady, fingertips on the envelope.
13.0s to 15.0s (hold): she keeps looking at Muthu; her lips press together and stay closed; her fingertips draw back 2 cm from the envelope and stop. The shot ends on her face.

THE HAND AND THE EYES (most important)
Her grief is shown only by small, physical things: the fingertips land on the envelope lightly, as if it were warm, and do not press; the thumb does not move. The eyes fill slowly at the lower rims from about 8.0s and the shine grows, but no tear falls; she blinks once, slowly, at the pause after the second sentence. Her lips tighten for a moment before the last sentence. Her voice never breaks into crying. No hand to the mouth, no wiping eyes with the pallu, no rocking, no looking up at the ceiling, no glance at the camera.

EYELINES
0.0s to 3.0s: Swathi looks at Muthu just right of the lens, level with her own eyes, head turned about 10 degrees to frame right. 3.0s to 5.6s: her gaze goes down to the envelope at the bottom of the frame, head tilted down about 25 degrees. 5.6s to 15.0s: her gaze returns to Muthu just right of the lens, level, and holds through the line and the end hold. She never looks into the lens and never toward the window or the clock.

DIALOGUE RULES
Dialogue rules: whenever a character speaks, frame them in a medium close-up with their face filling at least a third of the frame height. The speaking face is turned toward the camera, no more than 30 degrees from the lens, upright and not lying sideways. Only one person speaks in the shot; everyone else keeps their mouth closed. The speaker's head stays steady during the line; head turns, gestures and camera moves happen only in pauses between lines. The mouth is always fully visible, with nothing in front of the face, and the face is lit with soft, even frontal light. Natural, clearly articulated mouth movements matching the dialogue.

CAPTURE IMPERFECTIONS
0.0s to 15.0s: a very slight drift of a few millimetres in the frame as the camera breathes; 1.5s her shallow breath lifts the pallu about 5 mm; 4.0s a small focus breath as she leans 10 cm toward the table and back; 5.0s her slow breath in; 9.0s and 11.2s the short pauses inside the line, her breath audible; 12.0s a cloud passes the low sun and the light from the window dims slightly, then returns at 13.5s.

REAL-WORLD PHYSICS
The aerogramme sheets weigh a few grams each; a breath of air from the window lifts the edge of one unfolded letter about 3 mm and lets it fall. The envelope, about 40 g, does not slide under her fingertips. The cane chair creaks once as her weight comes forward. The faded curtain moves about 2 cm in the evening breeze through the bars. Nothing moves by itself; every movement has a cause.

PERFORMANCE
Quiet, private, very contained; a woman telling a stranger what she has told almost no one. Real unretouched skin with lines and dark circles, five fingers on each hand, eyes never on the lens. No posing, no theatrical weeping.

LIGHTING
Low late-afternoon sun at about 4000K through the thin curtain at frame left, a soft warm side light that is opened up with a gentle frontal fill at about 4500K so her face is even and readable; the room behind her falls to warm shadow; the wall clock's glass catches a faint glint. Never flat, never cold blue, never glossy.

AUDIO
Sound effects only — natural on-location sound, the soundtrack carries no music. No music at all: no score, no background music, no song, no humming, no riser, no whoosh. Only the sounds of this room and the lane outside.
0.0s to 15.0s: quiet room tone; a far street sound of a bicycle bell and a two-wheeler passing; a crow; the curtain brushing the bars; 3.0s to 5.6s the cane chair creaks and the paper whispers under her fingertips; 13.0s to 15.0s room tone and her breath.
Spoken language: Malayalam only. Swathi speaks one line in Malayalam, in Malayalam script exactly as written below, with a soft coastal Malayalam accent; dubbing language Malayalam. No English words, no translation, no subtitles.
6.0s to 13.0s — Swathi: [sad] "ബോധം വന്നപ്പോൾ ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നില്ല. ആശുപത്രിയുടെ പേപ്പർ ഇപ്പോഴുമുണ്ട്. അതു തുറന്നു നോക്കാൻപോലും വയ്യ."
Delivery: about 7.0 seconds, three sentences at a slow pace with a short pause after each full stop, low volume, pitch falling at the end of each sentence, the stress on "ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നില്ല" and on "വയ്യ"; the voice steady but thin, no sob.

STYLE
Photoreal 2026 Kerala feature film in Malayalam, 24fps, fine 35mm grain, gentle halation round the window, visible skin texture and real pores, no smoothing, no beauty filter. Shot on a Sony Venice 2 full-frame digital cinema camera: very wide dynamic range that holds the brightest light and the deepest shade in one frame; clean, low-noise shadows; soft, gradual highlight roll-off; accurate, natural colour with rich, true skin tones; gentle filmic contrast; fine, organic texture; no over-sharpening, no glossy or video look. Faded blue, lavender and warm amber palette.

CONSTRAINTS
Swathi alone in frame from the first frame to the last; no hand, shoulder or head of Muthu at the frame edges. The envelope stays closed; no papers taken out; no readable handwriting, stamps or print anywhere. No photograph of Selvan in this shot. Exactly one wall clock and one shelf with one steel jug; no religious pictures, no lamp, no idol anywhere in this room. No tears running down, no sobbing, no hand over the mouth. No camera move, no zoom, no cut. Only Malayalam is spoken; no English words. No lettering, no subtitles, no logo anywhere.

POSITIVE LOCKS
Exactly one person in frame: Swathi, once. Exactly one bundle of pale-blue letters with one loose white string, and one closed brown envelope on top, all on the low table; her right fingertips rest on the envelope from 3.0s to 13.0s. One wall clock, one shelf, one barred window. One Malayalam line, spoken once, from 6.0s to 13.0s, head steady, eyes just right of the lens. Locked 85mm medium close-up. Runs the full 15 seconds.

INTENT: Let it sound like something she has said to herself many times and almost never aloud: plain, slow, held together, with the weight on the paper she cannot open.
```
