Antony has just set the hurt Selvan down to sit on the sand against the shrine's front wall; as he straightens his own breath, he sees the plaster statue of Saint Anthony in the shrine's glass-fronted niche and, half surprised and half pleased, finds his own name in it. On it, alone, a tiny shrine: whitewashed lime plaster over coral stone, about 3 m wide, 2.5 m deep and 3 m high to the ridge of a low pitched roof of faded red clay tiles, a plain dark wooden cross about 60 cm high on the front of the ridge; the white walls stained grey and rust-brown by salt and rain near the ground. In its front wall, about 1.2 m above the sand, one small niche: a wooden box about 70 cm high and 45 cm wide, painted pale blue inside, with a small glass door in a thin wooden frame. The front of the shrine faces the sunrise side; Antony stands on the sea side, facing the shrine. Behind Antony: the flat sand falling to a narrow beach about 60 m away, the calm sea, a low, clean horizon; far out, about 300 m offshore, our uru at anchor, small and soft: grey-blue hull, one mast, the deckhouse aft, sails furled.
Light: the sun has not yet risen.
