Late afternoon, January 2005, the cemetery of a white Latin Catholic church on the sand of the Kerala coast. The emotional situation: Antony kneels alone at his mother's grave, pulls the grass away from her carved name with his fingers, and the grief he has held in finally breaks. The shot: one locked medium shot from low beside the grave, 15 seconds. Nobody speaks; there are no words, only his breathing and his crying. Emotion: crying.

Place: a parish cemetery on pale grey sand: rows of low graves about 2 m long, each a raised cement slab 20–30 cm high, whitewashed, weathered grey and black with lichen; each with a small whitewashed cement cross 50–70 cm tall at its head; narrow sand paths between the rows; tufts of coarse green grass and wild creepers growing round the slabs; three tall casuarina trees at the left with fine drooping needles. Behind, 40 m away and soft: the side of a white church with a tall pointed bell tower, its white walls gold in the late sun, and a low white compound wall.
Light: the sun low in the west, about 45 minutes before sunset, from the right of the frame; long shadows of the crosses falling to the left across the sand.
People in frame: Antony only: about 31, lean, brown skin; black hair cut short; a full black beard about 3 cm long hiding most of a pale scar on his LEFT jawline (on the right of the frame when he faces us); a plain dull-olive half-sleeve cotton shirt; faded dark-grey cotton trousers; worn black rubber sandals. No bag, no watch.
NOW: Antony kneels on the sand on both knees at the right side of his mother's grave, facing the camera at about 25 degrees, bent over the foot of the slab; both hands are already in the grass that has grown over the carved name.
