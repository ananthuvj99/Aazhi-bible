## Master prompt
```text
SCENE CONTEXT
Dawn, about 1985, the Alappad coast, Kerala: a Latin Catholic fishing village on a strip of sand between the Arabian Sea and the backwater. The night's fishing boats are coming home through the surf, and the village behind the beach wakes into its ordinary working day. Emotion: happy. The shot in one sentence: a high, wide view from over the shallow water that holds still, glides gently forward toward the beach as four wooden boats row in through the surf, and settles on the waking shore. Nobody speaks.

MEMORY (the coast of Antony's childhood; the same words in every shot of SB001–SB005)
Place: a long, gently curving beach of pale grey-gold sand running left to right across the frame; a low red laterite headland at the right third rising about 12 m above the beach, with a single-storey house on top among tall coconut palms (pale sky-blue walls, a hipped terracotta tile roof with a small chimney at the back, a low laterite compound wall), exactly as in @houseout; further along the shore at the right, a white church with a tall spire; behind the beach, rows of small clay-tiled cottages under coconut palms, sandy lanes between them, woven palm-leaf fences, thin blue wood smoke rising from kitchen roofs; low green hills far behind.
Light: the sun has just cleared the inland hills ahead of the camera, low and gold; the sea in the foreground carries a broad gold path; long soft shadows on the sand point toward the camera.
People in frame: the fishermen in the four boats, small; on the beach, small figures of women in cotton sarees and men in lungis walking down to the waterline with baskets; two small children running along the wet sand. Every face small and far; nobody close.

ACTIVE REFERENCES
@houseout (background only, 0.0s–15.0s): the house on the red laterite headland at the right third of the frame, its palms, its low laterite compound wall, the curving beach below it with the boats drawn up, and the white church with its tall spire further along the shore, exactly as in the reference, small and far in the dawn haze. Nobody stands at the house. 100% matches the reference.
@palmgreenwind (reference video, 0.0s–15.0s, movement only): exactly how coconut palm leaves sway, bend and flutter in a steady sea breeze. Give every palm along the shore that movement. Never copy its green background, its single tree or its look.

THE RETURNING VALLAMS
Four wooden fishing vallams of the 1980s Kerala coast, each about 10 m long and 1.5 m wide: plank-built hulls sewn and tarred black, a faded red and white band along the gunwale, a low pointed bow and stern, no cabin, no sail, no mast, no motor. Each carries five to six fishermen in faded lungis tied up at the knee, some bare-chested, some in old vests, two with cloth tied round the head. Long wooden oars, about 3.5 m, two or three each side, dipping together. A wet heap of brown cotton net in the middle of each boat, a few plaited palm-leaf baskets. The boats come in one behind another, about 25 m apart, from the lower left of the frame toward the beach in the middle. Never a modern fibreglass boat, never a ship, never a motorboat.

THE BOATS COMING HOME (most important)
The whole shot depends on the boats moving through real surf, each with a cause. 0.0s–2.5s: the nearest boat, lower left, rises over a small swell, its oars lifting out of the water together, water dripping from the blades; the other three are further in. 2.5s–6.0s: the oars dip and pull every 1.5 s; each pull pushes the boat forward about 1.5 m and a short white wake curls behind the stern. 6.0s–9.0s: the leading boat reaches the breaking surf; a wave about 0.8 m high lifts its stern, the men lean back and hold the oars out of the water, and the wave carries it in. 9.0s–12.5s: its bow runs up onto the wet sand with a small hiss of foam; two men jump out knee-deep and grab the gunwale; four figures from the beach hurry down to meet it. 12.5s–15.0s: the second boat rides the next wave; the men on the sand lean on the first boat's side, pushing it a little higher up the slope. No boat glides without oars or waves; no boat slides on sand by itself.

FORMAT MODE
One continuous shot, 15 seconds, real time, no cut, no slow motion. 21:9 cinematic. Runs the full 15 seconds.

CAMERA AND ACTION
0.0s–2.5s: the camera hangs about 25 m above the water, about 120 m out from the waterline, looking toward the shore and slightly down (about 10 degrees), level with the horizon line of the hills in the upper third; lens about 35mm; everything sharp from the nearest boat to the church. Held still. The first boat at the lower left, the beach across the middle, the headland house at the right third, the church far right, cottages and smoke across the back. The second, third and fourth boats further in, spread across the middle.
2.5s–12.5s: a gentle push forward and slowly down, at walking speed (about 1.5 m per second), so the camera ends about 105 m out and 18 m up; the beach and the cottages grow slowly in the frame through the travel alone; no turn, no tilt change beyond 5 degrees. The boats come in as written above. On the beach a woman sets a basket on her head; a man pulls a rope from a boat already drawn up; the two children run along the wet sand from left to right; smoke rises from three kitchen roofs and drifts inland.
12.5s–15.0s: the camera settles and holds, the shore filling the middle of the frame, the headland house still at the right third, the church far right. Sharp: the whole shore.

EYELINES
The fishermen in the boats look ahead at the beach and down at the water and their oars; the two men who jump out look at the gunwale under their hands. The people on the beach look out at the boats coming in; the woman with the basket looks down the slope at her feet. The two children look at the boats as they run. Nobody looks up at the camera.

CAPTURE IMPERFECTIONS
A very slow, slight sway in the frame all through, like a real body of air under it; at 6.0s a small exposure lift as the gold sun-path widens on the water; at 9.0s the foam where the first boat lands flares a little bright, then settles; a slight haze softens the hills and the church the whole time; spray from the oars catches the low sun in tiny bright points at 3.0s and 7.5s.

REAL-WORLD PHYSICS
A long, low swell about 0.5 m high every 6 seconds; the breaking surf about 0.8 m, white foam running 10–15 m up the sand and sliding back. Each vallam about 1.5 tonnes with its crew; an oar stroke about every 1.5 s pushing it about 1.5 m; the wave lifts the stern first, so the bow dips. Water streams off the oar blades. Wet sand darkens where the foam has been. Sea breeze from the sea toward the land about 15 km/h: palm fronds bend inland and flutter, the smoke leans inland, the women's saree ends lift. Nothing moves by itself; every movement has a cause.

PERFORMANCE
Ordinary working morning, unhurried and practised: men who do this every day, steady strokes, no shouting for the camera, no waving at the camera. Small, real bodies with real weight; bare feet in the foam; five fingers on every hand that grips a gunwale. Faces small, never posed.

LIGHTING
Low dawn sun about 3200K just above the hills ahead of the camera, backlighting the whole coast: a gold path on the water, gold rims on the boats, the oars and the men's shoulders; open shade on the sea-facing side of the boats about 6500K, soft blue; sky graded from warm apricot at the horizon to pale blue overhead; thin morning haze over the palms. Never orange all over, never noon-bright; the shadows long and soft.

AUDIO
Sound effects only — natural on-location sound, the soundtrack carries no music. No music at all: no score, no song, no humming, no cinematic sound design, no riser, no whoosh. No spoken words and no voices; every mouth stays closed or only breathes.
0.0s–15.0s: the steady wash of surf, gulls and terns crying, the breeze; 2.5s–9.0s the creak and knock of wooden oars in their rope loops and the splash of blades together; 9.0s the hiss of the bow running up the wet sand, two splashes of men jumping into the water; from far off on the shore, a rooster, a dog barking once, a distant church bell ringing three times slowly at 11.0s.

STYLE
Photoreal Kerala period feature film, about 1985, 24fps, fine 35mm grain, gentle halation around the low sun and the bright foam, visible skin texture and real pores on the nearest men, no smoothing. Shot on a Sony Venice 2 full-frame digital cinema camera: very wide dynamic range that holds the brightest light and the deepest shade in one frame; clean, low-noise shadows; soft, gradual highlight roll-off; accurate, natural colour with rich, true skin tones; gentle filmic contrast; fine, organic texture; no over-sharpening, no glossy or video look. Palette: warm gold, weathered black-tarred wood, pale sand, soft blue shade, green palms.

CONSTRAINTS
Known failures for this kind of shot: palms freezing in a moving shot (L157) — every palm crown moves with the breeze the whole time; boats drifting without oars or waves; modern boats with motors or fibreglass hulls; a sail or mast on the vallams; the house turning two-storey or gaining a flat roof; a second church; the camera turning away from the shore. Exactly one house on the headland, exactly one church. No lettering, no painted names, no flags, no plastic, no power cables in the foreground, no vehicles. No close faces. Nobody looks at the camera. No cut, no zoom.

POSITIVE LOCKS
One continuous 15-second shot. Exactly four vallams coming in, each once; the first lands at 9.0s, the second rides the surf by 15.0s. Exactly one house on the headland at the right third, exactly one white church far right. Every palm moving in the breeze. Smoke from exactly three kitchen roofs leaning inland. Two small children running along the wet sand. Low gold sun ahead. Nobody speaks. Runs the full 15 seconds.
```
